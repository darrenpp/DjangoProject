1. Purpose of the Report  
The purpose of this Situational Analysis Report is to assess the current internal and external 
environment of the Papua New Guinea Nursing Council (PNGNC). The report identifies 
strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT), key regulatory risks, and practical 
actions required to strengthen governance, systems, and legal defensibility.  
This report supports informed decision -making by the Board and guides priority reforms to 
ensure the Council operates as a strong, credible, and legally sound national regulator.  
2. Background and Context  
The PNG Nursing Council is the statutory body established in 1964 under the Medical Act 
1980 responsible for regulating the nursing and midwifery professions to protect public safety 
and maintain professional standards.  
The Council focused on strengthening its core regulatory and governance functions, which 
include:  
 Registration and licensing of nurses and midwives  
 Regulation of professional standards and scope of practice  
 Accreditation and monitoring of nursing and midwifery education programmes  
 Management of complaints, professional conduct, and disciplinary matters  
 Advisory role to key stakeholders on nursing and midwifery regulation and workforce 
issues  
A recent internal operational review has identified that while the Council has a strong 
mandate and committed leadership, many regulatory systems, documents, and processes 
have developed over time without a single, controlled governance framework.

The Council is currently operating under the Medical Act, which does not fully reflect modern 
nursing and midwifery regulatory practice. This limits the Council’s regulatory strength and 
creates legal and operational risks.   
  
3. Internal Environment Analysis  
3.1 Strengths  
• Clear national mandate to regulate nurses and midwives  
• Strong leadership commitment to reform and governance improvement  
• Increasing development of regulatory policies and standards  
• Experienced nursing and regulatory leadership  
• Active engagement in accreditation, registration, and standards setting  
• Strong relationships with key health sector stakeholders  
• Board oversight of major regulatory decisions  
3.2 Weaknesses  
• Outdated legislative framework  
• Limited and inconsistent regulatory policies and SOPs  
• Weak documentation control and version management  
• Manual and fragmented information systems  
• Weak audit trails for regulatory decisions  
• Inconsistent complaints and disciplinary handling  
• Reliance on individual practice rather than documented procedures  
• Limited staff capacity in regulatory best practice  
• Lack of formal case management system  
4. External Environment Analysis  
4.1 Opportunities  
• National health sector reform agenda  
• International regulatory standards (ICN, WHO) for benchmarking  
• Growing nursing and midwifery workforce  
• Increased public focus on patient safety and quality  
• Opportunity to develop a PNG Nursing Act from Health Practitioner Bill  
• Availability of digital systems to modernise regulation  
• Support from technical advisors and development partners 
• Opportunity to strengthen statutory autonomy of the Council to enhance regulatory 
independence, transparency, and accountability in line with international best practice. 
4.2 Threats  
• Legal challenges to Council decisions  
• Court scrutiny of due process and fairness  
• Stakeholder and political pressure on regulatory decisions

6. Key Strategic Risk: Legal Defensibility  
Risk Description  
There is a significant risk that some PNGNC regulatory decisions may not be fully defensible 
in court due to:  
• Lack of documented SOPs  
• Inconsistent regulatory processes  
• Weak documentation and audit trails  
• Informal or undocumented decision-making  
• Uncontrolled editing of Council regulatory documents  
• Reliance on outdated legislative authority

10. Way Forward (Phased Implementation Plan)  
Regulatory Strengthening Roadmap (2026 –2028). The Council’s 2026 priorities will be 
implemented through a phased regulatory strengthening program designed to progressively 
improve governance, regulatory systems, and legislative authority. 
 
Phase 1: Institutional Strengthening 
Short Term (0–6 Months) 
Focus: Establish foundational regulatory systems and governance structures 
Key Actions: 
1. Regulatory Governance 
• Review Council Board and Committee governance frameworks 
• Clarify committee roles, reporting lines, and decision-making processes 
 
2. Regulatory Documentation and Policy Systems 
• Approve Regulatory Documentation Management Policy 
• Review and standardise regulatory templates and official documentation 
3. Standard Operating Procedures 
Develop and implement core regulatory SOPs for: 
• Practitioner registration and licensing 
• Complaints management 
• Disciplinary procedures 
• Accreditation and inspection processes 
4. Case Management Framework

• Establish an integrated Case Management System (CMS) framework 
• Develop structured complaint intake and case tracking procedures 
5. Capacity Building 
• Train Council staff on regulatory SOPs and documentation protocols 
 
Phase 2: Systems Modernisation and Regulatory Oversight 
Medium Term (6–18 Months) 
Focus: Strengthen regulatory oversight and modernise operational systems 
Key Actions: 
1. Digital Regulatory Systems 
• Introduce digital practitioner registration and licensing systems 
• Implement electronic case tracking and regulatory data management tools 
2. Accreditation and Inspection Strengthening 
• Strengthen accreditation standards for nursing and midwifery education programs 
• Improve inspection and monitoring tools for training institutions 
3. Legislative Reform Preparation 
• Begin development and consultation process for the PNG Nursing Act 
• Conduct stakeholder consultations with: 
o National Department of Health 
o Provincial Health Authorities 
o Nursing education institutions 
o Professional associations 
4. Policy and Regulatory Framework Review 
• Review and update regulatory policies, guidelines, and administrative protocols 
 
Phase 3: Legislative Reform and Regulatory Maturity 
Long Term (18–36 Months) 
Focus: Achieve full regulatory authority and modern regulatory systems 
Key Actions: 
1. Enactment of PNG Nursing Act 
• Finalise legislative drafting 
• Secure government approval and parliamentary enactment 
2. Establish Regulatory Autonomy 
• Strengthen the Council’s statutory independence 
• Formalise governance arrangements under the new legislation 
3. Full Regulatory System Modernisation 
• Implement integrated national regulatory databases 
• Digitise practitioner records and licensing systems 
4. Alignment with International Standards 
• Align regulatory frameworks with global best practice standards, including those 
promoted by international nursing regulatory bodies 
Expected Outcomes 
Implementation of this roadmap will enable the Council to: 
• Strengthen regulatory governance and transparency 
• Improve practitioner registration and licensing systems 
• Establish structured complaints and disciplinary processes 
• Enhance accreditation and monitoring of nursing education programs 
• Achieve stronger statutory authority through new legislation 
• Align national regulatory systems with international best practice

11. Conclusion  
The situational analysis confirms that the Papua New Guinea Nursing Council operates with 
a clear national mandate and committed leadership; however, several structural, governance, 
and regulatory system gaps currently limit the Council’s ability to function as a fully modern 
and legally resilient regulator. Key challenges include reliance on outdated legislation, 
inconsistent regulatory procedures, weak documentation control, and limited digital systems. 
These weaknesses create potential risks to regulator y credibility, legal defensibility of 
decisions, and public confidence in the Council’s oversight of the nursing and midwifery 
professions. 
At the same time, the analysis identifies significant opportunities for reform. National health 
sector reforms, global regulatory standards, and the growing nursing workforce provide a 
strong foundation for strengthening the Council’s regulatory framework and institutional 
capacity. 
The implementation of structured governance systems, standardised policies and operating 
procedures, controlled regulatory documentation, modern information systems, and 
strengthened complaints and disciplinary processes will significantly improve the Coun cil’s 
operational effectiveness. Most importantly, the development and enactment of a dedicated 
Papua New Guinea Nursing Act will provide the legislative authority required to ensure 
regulatory independence, accountability, and alignment with international  regulatory 
practice. 
Through the phased regulatory strengthening roadmap outlined in this report, the Council 
can progressively transition toward a modern, transparent, and legally robust regulatory 
authority. This will enhance its ability to safeguard professional standards, protect public 
safety, and strengthen trust in the regulation of nursing and midwifery in Papua New Guinea.
