STATUTORY CONTEXT AND MANDATE OF THE PNG NURSING COUNCIL 
The Papua New Guinea Nursing Council is established under the Medical Registration Act 
1980 as the statutory authority responsible for regulating the nursing and midwifery 
professions in Papua New Guinea. 
The core regulatory functions of the PNG Nursing Council include: 
• Accreditation and monitoring of nursing and midwifery education programs 
• Registration and licensure of nurses and midwives 
• Maintenance of professional registers 
• Recognition of qualifications and approved specialisations 
• Setting and enforcing professional standards to protect public safety 
The Council does not hold statutory responsibility for: 
• Employment or payroll records 
• Deployment or posting of nurses 
• Workforce vacancies 
• Employment status (STC, permanent, acting, unattached) 
These functions fall under the National Department of Health, Provincial Health Authorities, 
and other employing authorities.

Regulatory Administrative Definition:    
▪ Provisional Licences are issued to newly graduated Papua New Guinea nurses and to overseas contractual nurses 
upon initial registration with the Council as probationary officers pending completion of the required supervised 
six (6) months competency period prior to eligibility for Full Registration.   
▪ Full Licences  are issued to Papua New Guinea nurses and overseas contractual nurses who have satisfactorily 
completed the mandatory six (6) months Competency Practicum and have met all registration requirements of 
the Council.     
▪ Renewal of registration for the issuance of Authority to Practise (ATP) is conducted on a biennial basis. Since 
2014, the Council has implemented a national synchronised renewal cycle, whereby all practising nurses and 
midwives are required to renew their registration in even -numbered years. Late renewals on uneven years are 
charged penalty fee of PGK100.00. Accordingly, the next national renewal cycle is scheduled for 2026.

REGULATORY OBSERVATIONS RELEVANT TO NATIONAL WORKFORCE 
PLANNING  
 
From a regulatory perspective, the Council notes the following: 
• Training outputs are constrained by accreditation standards (teacher: student 
ratio), clinical placement capacity (unregulated emergence of Nursing 
Institutions), and educator availability (Preceptors for clinical supervision). 
• Specialist nursing training remains limited and requires strategic expansion aligned 
with national service priorities, example the proposed oncology training  
• Workforce planning requires closer alignment between training capacity, 
regulatory approval, and employment planning 
• Strengthened coordination is needed to prevent misalignment between graduate 
numbers and health system absorption capacity.

WORKFORCE PLANNING CONSIDERATIONS  
While workforce deployment and staffing decisions rest with employing authorities, the PNG 
Nursing Council advises that: 
• National workforce planning should also be informed by regulatory training and 
registration data 
• Student intake quotas should be aligned with: 
o Accredited institutional capacity 
o Clinical training availability 
o Approved scopes of practice 
• Formal data-sharing mechanisms between PNGNC, NDoH, and PHAs would enhance 
workforce forecasting and planning accuracy. 
DATA LIMITATIONS 
The Papua New Guinea Nursing Council wishes to highlight the following limitations in 
relation to the data presented in this submission: 
1. Absence of a Modernized Registration System 
The Council currently operates without a fully modernized, integrated electronic 
registration and licensing system. As a result, workforce data is maintained through 
manual and semi-digital records, which limits real-time data accuracy, automation, 
and advanced analytics.  
2. No Online Registration and Real-Time Updating Capability 
The absence of an online registration and renewal platform constrains the Council’s 
ability to: 
o Capture real-time changes in registration status 
o Track workforce trends dynamically 
o Seamlessly integrate regulatory data with national workforce planning systems 
3. Data Scope and Depth Constraints 
Without a dedicated digital registry system, the Council’s data is limited to: 
o Registration and licensure status 
o Declared qualifications and recognized specializations 
o The system does not support routine real-time updates on employment 
movement, practice location, or service deployment.  
4. Resource and Sustainability Constraints 
The development, implementation, and ongoing operation of a modern regulatory 
registration system requires: 
o Dedicated skilled manpower (ICT, data management, regulatory officers) 
o Sustainable funding for system development, hosting, cybersecurity, 
maintenance, and user support such as high-tech computers (i7 Model) 
 
At present, these resource requirements are not fully resourced within the Council’s 
operational capacity.  
5. Implications for National Workforce Planning 
While the Council provides the most authoritative regulatory data on nursing and 
midwifery training and registration, the absence of a modernized system limits the

INDICATIVE TIMELINE FOR A MORE COMPREHENSIVE REGULATORY 
REPORT 
In accordance with the Ministerial request, the Papua New Guinea Nursing Council confirms 
that this submission constitutes an initial regulatory report, prepared using available training 
and registration data.  Subject to Ministerial direction, inter -agency collaboration, and 
resource availability, the Council advises that a more comprehensive regulatory analysis 
could be delivered in the following phases: 
Phase 1: Initial Regulatory Submission 
• Summary of training and registration data using existing records 
• Status: Completed by 23 January 2026 
Phase 2: Data Validation and Enhanced Disaggregation 
• Verification of active registration records 
• Improved classification of cadre and recognized specialization 
• Alignment of graduation and registration datasets 
• Indicative timeframe: 4–6 weeks 
Phase 3: Expanded Regulatory Analysis 
• Trend analysis of training outputs and registration patterns 
• Identification of regulatory gaps in specialist training pathways 
• Indicative timeframe: 6–8 weeks 
STRATEGIC REGULATORY REFORM RECOMMENDATIONS (PNGNC)  
To reposition and modernise the Papua New Guinea Nursing Council as a strong, credible, 
and effective statutory regulator, critical structural and regulatory gaps must be addressed to 
ensure institutional effectiveness, sustainability, and public protection. The following strategic 
regulatory reform recommendations are therefore presented for urgent consideration and 
implementation by the Council.    
1. Establish an Integrated Nurse Life-Cycle Registration and Workforce 
Information System 
It is strongly recommended that the Council: 
• Establish a fully integrated Nurse Life-Cycle Registration and Workforce Information 
System to ensure that each nurse and midwife has a single, continuous regulatory 
record from graduation through initial registration, provisional licensure, full 
registration, biennial ATP renewals, employment status changes, overseas practice, 
and eventual exit from the profession.

This system will: 
• Eliminate fragmented and duplicated data systems across registration, renewal, and 
workforce records 
• Enable real-time practitioner status verification for public protection 
• Strengthen monitoring of compliance, practice gaps, and licence validity 
• Support evidence-based workforce planning and national health policy advice 
• Enhance disciplinary and fitness-to-practise decision-making through complete 
practitioner histories 
2. Fund a National Accreditation Audit of All Schools of Nursing 
It is strongly recommended that the Council: 
• Secure a dedicated national budget to conduct a comprehensive accreditation status 
review of all Schools of Nursing. 
This is necessary to: 
• Address the growing emergence of unsanctioned and non-compliant nursing schools 
• Enforce national education standards and protect the quality of nursing graduates 
• Ensure all training institutions meet minimum infrastructure, faculty, clinical 
placement, and governance requirements 
• Restore regulatory authority and public confidence in nursing education standards 
3. Implement a National Regulatory Awareness Roadshow 
It is strongly recommended that the Council: 
• Conduct a national provincial roadshow to strengthen awareness of the Council’s 
regulatory mandate and professional expectations. 
This will: 
• Educate nurses, managers, educators, and students on registration, licensing, 
professional conduct, and scope of practice 
• Improve compliance with regulatory standards across all provinces 
• Rebuild professional discipline, ethics, and public accountability 
• Strengthen the Council’s visibility as a national regulator, not merely an administrative 
office 
4. Establish a National Nursing Workforce Data Integration Framework 
It is strongly recommended that the Council: 
• Formalise a national data-sharing framework requiring regular workforce data 
submissions from: 
o All accredited Schools of Nursing 
o Provincial Health Authorities (PHAs) 
o National Department of Health (NDOH)
